# SAFETY — read before you touch a reader

This is an authorized, controlled teaching environment. The same techniques are illegal against systems you don't own or have written permission to test. Act accordingly.

## Hard rules
1. **Your own blanks only.** You write a lab credential — the presenter's **default master fob** (their own, from a retired door-strike kit) or one you generate — onto **your own** blank cards. Never read, clone, or write anyone else's card, and never a real facility badge that isn't yours.
2. **No real badges.** Do not read, clone, or emulate anyone's actual access card, work badge, hotel key, or transit card — not your own, not a neighbor's.
3. **No attendee device is a target.** The Module 1 NDEF URL points to a **benign, real destination** (`https://adversaryvillage.org/`), or — for the optional Tier-1 beacon — a **local controlled host** you own. Payloads are non-destructive demonstrations only. You are not to point anything at another person's phone.
4. **Authorized use only, forever.** What you learn here is for engagements you are contracted and permitted to run. Cloning access credentials without authorization is a crime in essentially every jurisdiction.

## Code of Conduct
DEF CON and Adversary Village Code of Conduct apply at all times. Consent, respect, and "don't be a jerk" are not optional.

## RF & personal safety
- Keep readers flat and spaced; the floor is RF-noisy.
- If you have a pacemaker/ICD or other active medical implant, keep readers and magnets away from it and talk to a presenter before participating in proximity demos.

If you're not sure whether something is in bounds — **ask a presenter or pod lead before you do it.**
